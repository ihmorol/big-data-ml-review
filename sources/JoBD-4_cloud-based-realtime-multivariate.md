## Content (full text — every field corroborated by the PDF)

- **Problem / domain:** **real-time ICU vital-sign forecasting** — simultaneously predicting future **systolic blood pressure (SBP)** and **heart rate (HR)** to shift monitoring from reactive to proactive. Motivation: centralised cloud-only pipelines incur transmission delay and missing-data problems that are "not negotiable" in time-sensitive care (pp. 2–3, 12).
- **Big-data context:** genuine **velocity + volume streaming** framing — IoT sensors produce time-series streams requiring row-by-row processing; the system ingests them through **Apache Kafka** (broker) and processes them with **Apache Spark Streaming** (sliding windows) at the **fog layer**, with a cloud layer for historical storage/retraining. This is the corpus's clearest **platforms/pipelines** paper: Kafka + Spark + fog/cloud tiers, not just a model. Scale is one patient's minute-by-minute series (see Data); the "big data" claim rests on the *streaming architecture* rather than the dataset size. [evidence: PDF §"Background", §"Proposed FCMS-iDMM system"]
- **ML methods / architecture:** **FCMS-iDMM** — a fog/cloud medical system with an intelligent decision-making module, in **two phases**:
  - **Offline model development:** single-task (forecast HR *or* SBP) with **TCN, SLSTM/DLSTM, SGRU/DGRU**; multi-task (forecast HR *and* SBP jointly) with **TCN** and **seq2seq autoencoders** (A-SLSTM, A-DLSTM, A-SGRU, A-DGRU). Hyperparameters via **Optuna** (learning rate 1e-4–4e-4, units 32–256, dropout 0.1–0.5, filter 32–128, kernel 2–5); Adam, MSE loss, 30 epochs; input normalised to [−1,1] (MinMaxScaler).
  - **Online forecasting pipeline:** **simulated sensor → Kafka topic → Spark Streaming sliding window → deployed TCN**; sliding window 5 min input, 1 min slide, predicting **8 min ahead**.
  - **MTL objective:** minimise Σ wᵢ Lᵢ over shared layers (Eqs. 1–2).
  - Metrics: MSE, RMSE, MAE, MAPE; **paired p-values vs TCN** reported for LSTM/GRU comparisons. [evidence: PDF §"DL models based on fog computing", §"Experiments results"]
- **Data:** **MIMIC-III** (paper also writes "MIMIC II" in places — an inconsistency). SBP and HR extracted **minute-by-minute for two patients**; observation windows 5/10/15/20 min → target windows 8/15/20/25 min; split **80% train / 20% test**. Env: Intel i7-6700, RTX 4090, 16 GB, Python 11, Keras 3.8.0, TensorFlow 2.19.0, PyTorch 2.5.1. [evidence: PDF §"Data collection", Table 4 p. 22]
- **Key findings (with numbers as printed):**
  - **TCN is best across the board.** Multi-task 8-min forecast, patient 1: HR **RMSE 1.5428, MAE 1.0871, MAPE 1.269**; SBP **RMSE 4.1446, MAE 2.4323, MAPE 2.5237** — the headline numbers (Table 7 p. 31; abstract p. 1). Best seq2seq alternative (A-SGRU) was HR RMSE 1.721 / SBP 4.971.
  - **Worst model A-DLSTM** for 8-min: HR RMSE 4.361, SBP 6.090; in single-task HR, DLSTM RMSE 6.101 vs TCN 2.143 (Tables 5–7).
  - **Multi-task > single-task** across horizons (the two stated hypotheses both supported); TCN runs fastest too (e.g., 1 m 02 s vs 5+ min for seq2seq).
  - **Significance:** p-values for LSTM/GRU vs TCN are **<0.05**, reported as "statistically different from TCN, confirming that TCN's performance is significantly better" (pp. 26, 29, 33).
  - **Literature comparison** (Table 9, p. 39): the paper claims to be the only study combining **big-data platforms + fog/cloud + multitask**; competitors report e.g. CNN 6.5 RMSE, BI-LSTM 2.840, GRU 2.022 — none used fog/cloud.
- **Limitations the authors admit** (pp. 40–41): (1) **limited interpretability** — no XAI, though future work names SHAP/attention/Grad-CAM; (2) **centralised training phase** — federated learning suggested as future work; (3) **no real clinical deployment** — validated only on simulated sensors + retrospective MIMIC-III; (4) **hyperparameter optimisation** could be multi-objective (accuracy/latency/energy); (5) models **trained from scratch** (no transfer learning); (6) **single dataset** — external validation (eICU, VitalDB) needed; (7) **no transformer baselines** (TFT, Informer, Longformer); (8) **no metaheuristic hybridisation**; (9) **only SBP+HR** — other vitals excluded; (10) **potential dataset bias** — MIMIC-III is one US tertiary centre; demographic/ethnic/equipment bias unaddressed.
- **Limitations we see:** (a) **n = 2 patients** — the entire offline evaluation is two individuals, so generalisation claims rest on an extremely thin sample and the "significantly better" p-values are within-patient, not cross-patient; (b) **MIMIC-II vs MIMIC-III inconsistency** in the text (methods say MIMIC-II, abstract/results say MIMIC-III) creates a reproducibility ambiguity about which extraction was used; (c) the **real-time pipeline is tested on simulated sensors only**, so Kafka/Spark latency figures are architectural, not measured clinical latency; (d) **Table 9 lists "Our work … seq2seq (A-DGRU) … 1.721"** even though the paper's best model is TCN — a mislabelled comparison row that weakens trust in the table; (e) **no cross-patient or cross-site validation**, and the single 80/20 temporal split is not a speaker/patient-independent protocol; (f) the **"big data" label again rests on architecture** (Kafka/Spark) rather than data volume, which is defensible here but should be stated as such.
