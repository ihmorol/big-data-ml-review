## Content (full text — abstract confirmed against PDF)

- **Problem / domain:** The field conflates modern LLM-orchestrated agents with classical symbolic agents — "conceptual retrofitting" (describing LLM agents via BDI or perceive-plan-act loops) — which "obscures the true operational mechanics of LLM-based agents" and creates "a false sense of continuity between incompatible architectural paradigms" (pp. 2, 30). Application domains covered: healthcare, finance, robotics/automation, education, legal tech, scientific discovery, ethics/governance (pp. 16–21).
- **Big-data context:** No volume/velocity/throughput figures, no dataset sizes, no platform benchmarks — this is a conceptual survey, not a big-data systems paper. Its only scale claims are about compute and memory: neural agents show "extreme dependence on vast, expensive cloud compute"; "context window limitations create agents with severe amnesia across sessions. Statelessness prevents cumulative learning"; symbolic POMDP belief tracking has "significant computational overhead" that "limits their scalability" (pp. 6, 25). Imperatives named: model distillation, sparse architectures, hybrid cloud-edge deployment (p. 25).
- **ML methods / architecture:** Two lineages. Symbolic/classical: MDP (S,A,P,R), POMDP belief states, BDI and SOAR cognitive architectures (pp. 6–7); multi-agent coordination via contract net protocol, blackboard systems, market-based allocation, with JADE, JaCaMo, early SOAR (Table 8, p. 22). Neural/generative: deep RL (PPO, meta-DRL) as the bridge, then LLMs as a "general-purpose substrate for reasoning based on statistical prediction" (pp. 7–8); orchestration via prompt chaining (LangChain), multi-agent conversation (AutoGen), role-based workflow (CrewAI), plugin composition (Semantic Kernel), RAG (LlamaIndex), dynamic context management/state machines (LangGraph) (Tables 3, 7; pp. 9, 20, 22).
- **Review method:** PRISMA 2020 (p. 10). Databases: IEEE Xplore, ACM DL, arXiv, SpringerLink, ScienceDirect, Google Scholar (p. 11). Window Jan 2018 – Mar 2025, English only; generative-AI-only work without agency excluded (pp. 12, Table 4 p. 13). Screening: 165 items (157 database + 8 supplemental) → 120 after dedup → 42 excluded on title/abstract → 78 core studies → + 12 seminal symbolic/theoretical papers = **final corpus of 90 publications** (pp. 12–13, Fig. 7 p. 14). Thematic synthesis (Thomas & Harden 2008), two independent coders, inter-coder reliability on a 15% subset, **Cohen's Kappa = 0.82**, NVivo (p. 13).
- **Paper structure:** §1 Introduction pp. 2–5 · §2 dual-paradigm framework pp. 5–9 (2.1 p. 6; 2.2 symbolic details pp. 6–7; 2.3 DRL/LLM pp. 7–9; 2.4 orchestration p. 9) · §3 Methodology pp. 10–15 (limitations §3.6 pp. 15–16) · §4 Findings pp. 15–23 (4.1 p. 16; 4.2 pp. 16–19; 4.3 p. 19; 4.4 pp. 19–21; 4.5 pp. 21–22; 4.6 evaluation p. 23) · §5 Discussion pp. 24–30 (limitations §5.5 pp. 29–30) · §6 Conclusion p. 30 · References pp. 31–36.
- **Proposed framework/taxonomy:** Dual-paradigm taxonomy (Fig. 2, p. 5): symbolic/classical ("algorithmic planning and persistent state") vs neural/generative ("stochastic generation and prompt-driven orchestration"), described as having "fundamentally incompatible" mechanisms. Expanded in Fig. 8 (p. 16) into seven colour-coded domains plus five historical eras; all 90 studies classified in Table 5 (pp. 17–18). Operationalised through Table 8 (MAS coordination, p. 22), Table 9 (nine research-gap areas, pp. 25–26), Table 10 (strategic trajectories, p. 27), Table 11 (six ethical/governance challenges, p. 29).
- **Key findings / claims:**
  - Paradigm choice is strategic, not a quality ranking: high-stakes regulated domains (healthcare, legal) favour symbolic or constrained neural designs; data-rich adaptive domains (finance) favour neural orchestration (pp. 1, 16, 24).
  - Governance divide: ethics/governance work focuses almost entirely on neural-paradigm risks, leaving "a significant gap in modernized governance frameworks for purely symbolic systems" (pp. 16, 24).
  - Temporal shift: symbolic and hybrid architectures dominated 2018–2021; "neural Orchestration Frameworks have overwhelmingly dominated post-2022, following the rise of LLMs" (p. 16).
  - Evaluation must be paradigm-aware: symbolic systems judged on verifiability, neural on robust adaptability, because "Simple metrics like accuracy are wholly insufficient"; agency "is not a monolithic property but a spectrum" (p. 23).
  - Both lineages remain flawed: neural systems are "plagued by opaqueness, an inability to perform verifiable reasoning, and a massive dependence on unsustainable compute infrastructure"; symbolic systems stay brittle in open-world settings (pp. 24–26). Neuro-symbolic integration is named "the keystone" future direction (pp. 26, 30).
- **Author-stated limitations:** Search window ends early 2025; the 12 supplemental seminal symbolic papers "represent a deviation from a purely systematic retrieval process"; proprietary systems meant "architectural details and performance metrics were sometimes incomplete or inferred from secondary sources"; methodological heterogeneity prevented cross-study benchmarking; classification of hybrid systems "may, in some cases, involve necessary simplification" (pp. 15–16, 29–30). Open challenges (Table 9, pp. 25–26; Table 11, p. 29): no paradigm-specific benchmarks (AgentBench/GAIA "fail to adequately test for subtle misalignments, prompt robustness, and the true cost of context management"); memory/statelessness; unsustainable cloud dependence; HCI and trust gaps; prompt injection, goal drift, value misgeneralization; the neural "attribution gap" in liability; missing interoperability standards; hybrid systems that "inherently inherit the governance challenges of both" (p. 28).
- **Limitations we see:** the dual-paradigm taxonomy is asserted, not tested — the successful classification of all 90 studies is self-reported (the κ = 0.82 check covers only the coding subset, not the final classification); the "unsustainable compute" argument is qualitative — no token/cost/latency measurements are given; 12 of the 90 corpus items entered through a non-systematic supplemental phase, so ~13% of the evidence bypassed the PRISMA screen; and domain coverage is skewed to text-based LLM agents — nothing on agents operating over distributed/streaming big-data infrastructure, which is exactly the angle our review would want.
- **Datasets / benchmarks / systems:** None run — evidence base is the 90-paper corpus. Benchmarks discussed critically: AgentBench, GAIA (pp. 23, 25, 27). Systems named: AutoGPT, CrewAI, AutoGen (p. 4); LangChain, Semantic Kernel, LlamaIndex, LangGraph (pp. 9, 20, 22); JADE, JaCaMo, early SOAR (p. 22); MYCIN, DENDRAL (p. 3); IBM Governance Stack, Sakana AI Scientist, Microsoft Discovery (Table 10, p. 27). Application exemplars from corpus: breast-ultrasound report generation with LangChain (p. 20), MedAide on-premise edge medical assistance (p. 20), CrewAI stock-risk analysis and FinLlama RAG financial sentiment (pp. 17, 20–21).
- **Table 1 row data:** Agentic AI (survey; symbolic vs neural paradigms) | Symbolic: MDP, POMDP, BDI/SOAR, contract net/blackboard; neural: deep RL (PPO), LLM orchestration (prompt chaining, multi-agent conversation, role-based workflow, plugin composition), RAG | Not reported — argues cloud-compute dependence is unsustainable; calls for distillation, sparse architectures, hybrid cloud-edge deployment (p. 25) | Not reported — no datasets or benchmarks; evidence base is 90 publications (78 core + 12 seminal), Jan 2018–Mar 2025 (pp. 12–14) | Introduces a dual-paradigm taxonomy (symbolic vs neural/generative) to end "conceptual retrofitting", PRISMA-reviewing 90 studies to show paradigm choice is domain-driven and the path forward is neuro-symbolic.

## Synthesis hooks

- **Theme placement:** Agentic AI & RAG (see notes/landscape-report.md taxonomy) — frames RAG as one of five neural orchestration mechanisms that "replaces internal symbolic knowledge bases with on-demand, external context retrieval" (Tables 3 and 7, pp. 9, 20) and as a way to ground stochastic generation in finance (pp. 20–21) and constrain neural legal agents (Table 5, p. 17).
- **Agrees with:** TBD-2 and TBD-3 — the LLM/foundation model as the new reasoning substrate and orchestration (not task-specific models) as the emerging design pattern (pp. 8–9); AIR-3 — the classical-ML → deep → foundation-model arc and domain-constrained architecture choice (pp. 3–4, 16, 20–21); AIR-5 — shared critique that "varied evaluation metrics across studies limited our ability to perform direct cross-study benchmarking" (p. 15); AIR-4 — governance and data-quality frameworks lag capability in high-stakes healthcare (pp. 16, 28).
- **Contradicts:** Nothing in the pool is directly contradicted (no falsifiable empirical claim), but it contrasts sharply in evidence type with every experimental paper (TBD-1, TBD-2, BDR-3, BDR-5, JoBD-1, JoBD-4): they report datasets, platforms and metrics; this reports none. It also pushes against the pool's default assumption that scaling neural models is the answer — arguing against "unsustainable compute" and favouring symbolic/hybrid designs in safety-critical deployments (pp. 16, 24–26).
- **Extends/enables:** BDR-1, TBD-5, JoBD-5, AIR-2 (security/anomaly detection) — Table 11 supplies an agentic threat model and evaluation dimensions those papers lack; BDR-2, JoBD-2 (federated/privacy) — its decentralised cloud-edge efficiency imperative motivates their deployment settings; BDR-3, TBD-1, BDR-5, JoBD-1 (platforms/scalable ML) — its tool-composition and RAG mechanisms show conceptually how agentic layers wrap big-data pipelines as callable tools; JoBD-3 — prompt-driven orchestration as the same mechanism class extended to multimodal pipelines.
- **Unique contribution:** Only pool paper offering a dual-paradigm taxonomy plus the "conceptual retrofitting" critique as an analytical instrument, backed by a PRISMA-2020 review of 90 studies with a reported inter-coder reliability (κ = 0.82), and the only one supplying paradigm-differentiated governance, ethics and research-gap tables (Tables 9–11) instead of experimental results.
- **Quotable line:** "symbolic and hybrid architectures dominate safety-critical applications like healthcare and robotics, while pure neural systems thrive in data-rich, adaptive domains such as finance and education." (p. 24) · *alt:* "The future of Agentic AI lies in the strategic integration of neuro-symbolic architectures that leverage the complementary strengths of symbolic reliability and neural adaptability." (p. 30)
