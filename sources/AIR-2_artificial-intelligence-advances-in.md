## Content (full text — abstract confirmed against PDF)

- **Problem / domain:** AI-driven anomaly detection for telecom networks (faults, DDoS attacks, fraud, service degradation), aimed at 5G/6G, edge computing and IoT contexts. Rule-based/threshold detection is declared obsolete — unscalable, high false positives/negatives, reactive only (pp. 2, 4; Table 1 p. 5).
- **Big-data context:** Scale claims are qualitative, not quantitative. "Massive amounts of data" (p. 1); Fig. 1 "Data Growth Worldwide 2010–2024" used as the justification for AI integration (p. 3); telecoms "generate vast amounts of data continuously from various sources, such as user activity, network equipment logs, and traffic patterns" (p. 14); "velocity of data creation demands that AI models process inputs rapidly" (p. 29). The only platform mention is historical: "2010s–2020s: Big Data & Real-Time Analytics — Integration with big data technologies (Hadoop, Spark)" with limitations "real-time processing complexities / high infrastructure costs" (Table 1, p. 5). No records/day, traffic volume or byte-scale figures anywhere.
- **ML methods / architecture:** A catalogue, not one architecture. Classical: SVM/SVR, one-class SVM, k-NN, LOF, CBLOF, PCA, t-SNE, Isolation Forest, DBSCAN, k-means, Random Forest, XGBoost, SARIMA, S-H-ESD, STAMP (pp. 8, 13; Tables 2, 5). Deep: CNN, RNN/GRU, LSTM, autoencoder/DAE, EncDec-AD, LSTM-VAE, VAE-GAN, MSCRED, DeepAnT, OmniAnomaly, Telemanom, TCN-AE, DBN, SNN, SOM (Table 2 pp. 9–12; Table 3 pp. 20–21). Emerging: GNNs, GATs, TGNs, transformers/attention, self-supervised, meta-/few-shot, contrastive learning, Bayesian NNs, RL, XAI, federated learning (Table 3 pp. 20–21; §5.1 pp. 19–22). Recommended direction: hybrids (DL + domain rules), GAN/VAE synthetic rare-anomaly data, federated learning + edge AI (pp. 1, 16, 22, 33).
- **Review method:** PRISMA. Databases: Springer, IEEE, ScienceDirect, Google Scholar, arXiv; keyword combination AI/ML/DL, anomaly detection, telecom networks, network security, 5G (pp. 17–18). Counts (p. 18, Fig. 5): 3,084 gathered → 1,434 duplicates excluded → 1,258 removed on publication-year criteria → 232 excluded (inaccessible/irrelevant/insufficient quality) → **160 articles included**. No date range, no per-database counts, no dual-screening/kappa reported.
- **Paper structure:** §1 Introduction pp. 2–4 · §2 Historical context pp. 4–7 (Table 1 timeline 1960s–2024, p. 5) · §3 Overview of AI techniques pp. 7–16 (Table 2 pp. 9–12; evaluation pp. 8–14; contextualisation pp. 14–16) · §4 Methodology (PRISMA) pp. 17–18 · §5 Recent advances pp. 19–23 (Table 3 pp. 20–21; case studies §5.2 pp. 22–23) · §6 Empirical data pp. 23–32 (6.1 datasets pp. 24–25; 6.2 metrics + Table 5 p. 26; 6.3 pp. 27–29; 6.4 challenges pp. 29–32) · §7 Conclusion + recommendations pp. 32–33 · References pp. 34–40.
- **Key findings / claims:**
  - Deep learning dominates: CNN/RNN/LSTM/autoencoders capture non-linear and temporal dependencies where k-NN/LOF/PCA struggle with scale and dimensionality (pp. 8, 13, 27). Reported detection quality clusters at 85–97% (DeepAnT 91%, LSTM-AD 92%, LSTM-VAE 92%, MTAD-GAT 93%, MSCRED 91%, NumentaHTM 94%, Telemanom 90%, STAMP 90% recall, S-H-ESD 85%) (Table 4, p. 24).
  - Accuracy is not free — Table 5 (p. 26) frames an accuracy-vs-latency/scalability trade-off: autoencoders 85–95% at 2,000–7,000 ms; LSTM 80–95% at 1,000–5,000 ms; Isolation Forest 80–90% at 100–400 ms with high scalability; XGBoost 85–97% at 300–1,000 ms (pp. 26–27).
  - Unsupervised/reconstruction methods are the default because labelled telecom anomalies are scarce, rare and imbalanced (pp. 8, 24, 30); label acquisition is the supervised bottleneck (pp. 6, 8).
  - Emerging techniques diversify the field: GNN/GAT/TGN for topology and time-evolving graphs, transformers for long-range dependencies, federated + edge for privacy/bandwidth/latency, self-supervised/meta/few-shot to cut label dependence, GAN/VAE for rare-anomaly synthesis, XAI for transparency, RL/BNN for dynamics and uncertainty (Table 3 pp. 20–21; §5.1 pp. 19, 22).
  - Deployment, not model accuracy, is the blocker: GPU/TPU and cloud egress/storage costs, edge resource constraints and latency, 5G/6G-scale heterogeneity and the retraining/model-drift burden (pp. 29–32).
- **Author-stated limitations:** §6.4 (pp. 29–32): computational limitations, model interpretation, data availability and quality, scalability, pre-/post-processing requirements; noisy imbalanced data with rare anomalies (p. 30); real-time processing straining on-premises infrastructure (pp. 29–30); model drift and continuous-retraining cost/disruption (p. 30); accuracy loss when scaling (p. 15). Own scope limit: "No datasets were generated or analysed during the current study" (p. 33). Six research recommendations (pp. 32–33): hybrid models for accuracy + explainability; real-time/scalable edge and distributed AI; better preprocessing (PCA, noise filtering, synthetic data); federated learning + edge AI for privacy compliance; standard benchmarks and evaluation metrics; long-term self-adapting/continual models.
- **Limitations we see:** The performance numbers in Tables 4–5 are aggregate, unattributed to named datasets or code, and self-reported — indicative, not reproducible evaluations (contrast TBD-5, which reports per-method results on three named datasets). No volume/velocity figures support the "big data" framing (no records/day, no GB/TB, no streams/sec), and the Hadoop/Spark mention is historical only (Table 1, p. 5). Not a single public benchmark is named (no KDD/NSL-KDD/CICIDS/NAB/SMD), and no telecom operator data is used — the case studies are secondary citations (pp. 22–23). No LLM/foundation-model content at all, a real thematic gap given TBD-2/TBD-3/AIR-3. Production is sloppy: reference years contain obvious typos ("EncDec-AD (Malhotra et al. 1607)", pp. 11 and 24; "Torsk (Heim and Avery 1909)" p. 10; "Michelucci 2201" p. 11; "Parnami and Lee 2203" p. 20) — verify any borrowed citation.
- **Datasets / benchmarks / case studies:** No dataset used by the authors (§6.1 pp. 24–25 only categorises datasets from reviewed studies): (1) 5G streams (latency, throughput, signal strength, packet loss) for STAMP/DeepAnT/MSCRED; (2) telecom infrastructure sensor telemetry (temperature, power, signal quality) for NumentaHTM/LSTM-AD; (3) intrusion/security logs (DDoS, auth attempts, alerts) for S-H-ESD/Telemanom/EncDec-AD; (4) network traffic logs (volume, routing, bandwidth); (5) synthetic/benchmark sets for anomaly injection. Case studies §5.2 (pp. 22–23), all secondary: real-time DDoS mitigation (Saeed et al. 2023), optical-transport fault localisation (Rafique & Velasco 2018), mobile-network performance monitoring (Umoga et al. 2024). Further examples: federated autoencoder/LSTM training at the 5G edge (p. 15); LSTM autoencoder on service-provider logs (Vlk 2024, p. 15); LSTM-AE + one-class SVM for latency/packet-loss (p. 15); attention CNN on mobility traffic (p. 19); capsule networks on network logs (p. 19); KBES + DL on cellular networks (p. 19); unsupervised IoT anomaly detection (p. 19).
- **Table 1 row data:** Telecom network anomaly detection (DDoS/security, fault management, service quality) | CNN, RNN/LSTM/GRU, autoencoders, VAE/VAE-GAN, GANs, RL, GNN/GAT/TGN, transformers/attention, SVM, k-NN, LOF, Isolation Forest, Random Forest, XGBoost, ensembles/hybrids, federated learning | Hadoop/Spark named only historically (Table 1, p. 5); edge computing, federated learning, microservices/containerisation discussed; no platform used experimentally | No datasets analysed by the authors (p. 33); five dataset categories described but no named public dataset and no size/volume figures | PRISMA review (3,084 screened → 160 included) charting the rule-based → deep-learning transition in telecom anomaly detection, cataloguing methods, case studies, reported accuracies/latencies and deployment barriers; recommends hybrid, self-adaptive, edge/federated systems.

## Synthesis hooks

- **Theme placement:** Security & anomaly detection (see notes/landscape-report.md taxonomy) — the survey arm of the pool's anomaly-detection vertical alongside TBD-5 (streaming methods) and JoBD-5 (graph methods); frames the theme historically and operationally (rule-based ops → AI, spanning DDoS/fraud, fault management, service quality) for 5G/6G/edge/IoT (pp. 1–3, 22–23, 31).
- **Agrees with:** TBD-5 — unsupervised/deep reconstruction as the right default under scarce labels and imbalance, and the adaptive/self-adaptive direction (pp. 8, 24, 30, 32–33); JoBD-5 — relational/topological structure helps network anomaly detection (GNN/GAT/TGN credit, p. 20); BDR-2 and JoBD-2 — federated learning as the privacy-preserving route to distributed training (pp. 14–15, 30, 33); BDR-1 — explainability as a first-class requirement (Table 3 p. 21; recommendation 1 p. 33); AIR-5 — deep LSTM/AE/transformer models dominate multivariate time-series anomaly detection (Tables 2, 4); TBD-1 — tree ensembles (RF, XGBoost, Isolation Forest) as the accuracy-plus-scalability family (Table 5, p. 26).
- **Contradicts:** No pool paper contradicts its narrative (rule-based → ML → DL/hybrid); the tension is evidential. It contrasts with AIR-5, which is an empirical reproducibility audit (AIR-2 runs no experiments and its Table 5 ranges are dataset-unattributed) and with TBD-5/JoBD-5/BDR-3/BDR-5, which all report measured results on named data or platforms while AIR-2 explicitly states "No datasets were generated or analysed" (p. 33) and quantifies no scale. It also asserts iForest/Random Forest suit large real-time datasets (p. 14) without measurement — exactly what TBD-1 and BDR-5 test experimentally.
- **Extends/enables:** TBD-2, TBD-3, AIR-3 — extend the transformer/time-series branch AIR-2 identifies (Table 3, p. 20) into LLM/foundation-model territory AIR-2 omits entirely, giving those papers a domain gap to be framed against; TBD-4 — supplies the methodological depth behind AIR-2's long-range-dependency claim (p. 20); AIR-4 — extends its GAN/VAE synthetic-data recommendation (pp. 16, 20–22, 30) into data-quality/governance; BDR-3 and BDR-5 — operationalise the Hadoop/Spark-era integration AIR-2 names only historically (Table 1, p. 5) and the accuracy-at-scale question it leaves open (pp. 15, 27); JoBD-4 — concrete cloud/fog streaming architecture for AIR-2's edge/real-time recommendations; BDR-4 — extends its fraud-detection application catalogue (pp. 8, 14) into financial graphs.
- **Unique contribution:** The only telecom-network paper in the pool and the only one with a historical evolution timeline (1960s manual thresholds → 1980s rules → statistical/ML → big-data Hadoop–Spark era → 2020s DL; Table 1, p. 5), plus a PRISMA-scoped (3,084 → 160) telecom-specific catalogue of named algorithms with reported accuracies, computation times, scalability ratings and dataset categories, telecom case studies, and the pool's only treatment of operator-facing deployment economics (GPU/TPU and cloud egress costs, model drift, retraining) for 5G/6G (pp. 29–32).
- **Quotable line:** "Traditional methods of anomaly detection, which rely on rule-based systems, are no longer effective in today's fast-evolving telecom landscape."
