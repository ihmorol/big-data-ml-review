## Content (full text — every field corroborated by the PDF)

- **Problem / domain:** **privacy-preserving collaborative healthcare AI** — medical-image classification across multiple institutions without sharing raw patient data. Motivation: > **30% of healthcare organizations worldwide suffered a data breach in the past year**; conventional centralised training pools sensitive data and creates compliance/leakage risk (p. 2, abstract p. 1).
- **Big-data context:** framed as **Big Data in healthcare** — many-centre data with **non-IID heterogeneity** (differing demographics, imaging protocols, disease prevalence, equipment). Scale is *institutional*, not petabyte: **3 datasets, 10 simulated clients**, with extreme class imbalance injected in clients 2, 5, 8 (p. 15). Platform: a **simulated multi-centre FL environment** (multi-cloud simulated, not actually deployed). Privacy stack: **differential privacy** in the centralised arm and **federated learning** (FedAvg / FedSGD / proposed adaptive) in the decentralised arm; optional SMPC via Shamir's Secret Sharing. **No Spark/Hadoop** named; this is an institution-scale, not machine-scale, big-data paper. [evidence: PDF §1, §"Data and methodology", §"Federated learning setup"]
- **ML methods / architecture:** **Adaptive FL aggregation** — per communication round the server computes a divergence statistic δ_t = (1/K)Σ‖w_k^t − w_global^t‖₂ (the average Euclidean distance of local from global updates) and switches: if δ_t > τ use **FedSGD** (finer gradient-based updates), else **FedAvg** (efficient weighted averaging). **τ = 0.10** selected empirically (τ<0.08 over-uses FedSGD; τ>0.15 delays convergence) (pp. 20–24, Table 10 p. 38). Local models: **ResNet (27 MB, 6.8 M params)** and **VGG16 (552 MB, 138 M params)**, both ImageNet-pretrained and fine-tuned (transfer learning). Regularisation: dropout + early stopping; loss = categorical cross-entropy. Centralised arm uses **differential privacy** (Gaussian noise on gradients, σ²=0.5, ε≈2.5 chosen); optional **SMPC (3,10)/(5,10)** on client updates. A **Chi-square uniformity test** validates the SMOTE-balanced client distribution (p. 15). A Theorem 1 + Corollary give an optimal-dynamic-aggregation statement based on gradient divergence (pp. 25–26).
- **Data:** three public Kaggle datasets, resized to 224×224:
  - **TB Chest X-ray** — **7,000** images (3,500 normal / 3,500 TB).
  - **Diabetic Retinopathy** — **3,662** JPEG (5 classes: No_DR, Mild, Moderate, Severe, Proliferate_DR).
  - **Brain Tumor MRI** — **3,624** JPEG (4 classes: No Tumor, Meningioma, Glioma, Pituitary).
  - Split 70% / 15% / 15% train/val/test; distributed across **10 clients in non-IID fashion**; SMOTE to counter imbalance. Metrics: accuracy, precision, recall, F1; RMSE for training error; SHAP for feature-importance transparency. [evidence: PDF Table 3 p. 13, §"Non-IID data handling"]
- **Key findings (with numbers as printed):**
  - **Adaptive aggregation reaches up to 96.3% accuracy**, comparable to FedAvg/FedSGD, while **reducing execution time ≈20%** and cutting communication rounds ≈20% (abstract p. 1; Table 9 p. 37: **adaptive 320 s / 15 epochs** vs FedAvg 360 s / 18 and FedSGD 400 s / 20).
  - **Centralised+DP is more accurate** than any federated setting: **96.0%–98.3%** (e.g., ResNet TB 97.9% normal, 96.4% TB); federated FedAvg 94.9%–95.9%; adaptive sits between FedSGD and FedAvg and **beats both** on non-IID cases (Diabetic Retinopathy No_DR: adaptive ResNet **96.3%** vs FedAvg 96.1% / FedSGD 95.8%) (Tables 6–8, pp. 29–33).
  - **Privacy–utility trade-off** (Table 11, p. 39): σ²=0.1 → 97.4% acc / ε=5.0 (high privacy); σ²=0.5 → 96.5% / ε=2.5 (balanced, chosen); σ²=1.0 → 94.5% / ε=1.0 (very strong privacy, 50 epochs). Adaptive+DP+SMPC → 94.5%, "very high" privacy/robustness.
  - **Scalability** (Table 19, p. 47): 10 clients (TB 95.5%, brain 96.2%, DR 95.7%, 280 s) → 20 clients (90.8%, 91.5%, 91.7%, 320 s): accuracy degrades ~4–5 pts, convergence time rises modestly.
  - **Communication overhead** (Table 16, p. 42): VGG16 ≈ **276 GB total over 50 rounds** (5,520 MB/round; 552 MB per client per round) vs ResNet **13.5 GB** (270 MB/round) — a 20× gap from parameter count alone.
- **Limitations the authors admit** (p. 50, §"Conclusion and future prospects"): **communication overhead remains a challenge** under limited bandwidth/high latency; **client-level computational constraints may limit scalability as dataset size grows**; **real multi-cloud deployment not tested** — only simulated (p. 52); and the Table 2 self-row notes "computational overhead was significant for larger models like VGG-16."
- **Limitations we see:** (a) **all federation is simulated** — the "multi-centre" setting is one machine partitioning one dataset; the paper acknowledges this only in future work, so **real network latency/dropout/privacy-in-transit are unevidenced**; (b) **differential privacy was applied only to the centralised arm, not to the federated rounds**, so the headline "privacy-enhanced" claim for the FL setting rests on data locality, not on a DP/SMPC guarantee in the main results (SMPC is evaluated only as a side table, Table 12–13); (c) the **"adaptive" gain is modest and partly untested statistically** — no significance test compares adaptive vs FedAvg accuracy, and Table 9's 320 s vs 360 s gap is a single win, not a distribution; (d) **SMOTE resampling on medical images** is methodologically risky and is applied before the non-IID partition, which can leak synthetic neighbours across client boundaries and inflate robustness; (e) **datasets are small-to-moderate (~14k total images), not "Big Data" by volume**, echoing JoBD-1's loose use of the term; (f) the divergence threshold τ=0.10 is tuned on this setup and may not transfer.
