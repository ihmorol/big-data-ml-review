## Content (full text — abstract confirmed against PDF)

- **Problem / domain:** Multimodal data integration in oncology and cancer research (genomics, transcriptomics, proteomics, digital histopathology whole-slide images [WSIs], radiology [CT/MRI/PET], and clinical health records) for diagnosis, prognosis, risk stratification, and personalized therapeutic selection.
- **Big-data context:** Extreme heterogeneity, volume, and dimensionality. Single-cell datasets span millions of cells (e.g., scGPT trained on >33M cells; Nicheformer on 110M cells); digital histopathology WSIs are gigapixel-scale images; multi-omics profiles contain tens of thousands of continuous and discrete dimensions. Key systems bottlenecks: high memory consumption during multi-view fusion, high compute/GPU requirements for foundation model pretraining, and lack of standardized data pipelines across clinical institutions.
- **ML methods / architecture:** Traces a three-generation evolution:
  1. *Classical ML:* SVM, Random Forest, XGBoost, Canonical Correlation Analysis (CCA), multi-view kernel learning, sparse representation.
  2. *Deep Learning:* CNNs (ResNet, DenseNet for pathology/radiology), Autoencoders (VAE for multi-omics dimensionality reduction), Graph Neural Networks (GNNs, hypergraphs for protein-protein interaction [PPI] and gene regulatory networks), Cross-modal Attention/Transformers.
  3. *Foundation Models (FMs):* Self-supervised and contrastive pretrained models—scGPT (single-cell transformer), Nicheformer (spatial transcriptomics), OmniCLIP (paired WSIs + spatial transcripts via CLIP-style dual encoders), GET (regulatory grammar), and MolFM (molecular structures + text).
- **Two-Tiered Fusion Taxonomy:**
  - *Tier 1 (Timing of Integration):* Early fusion (input feature concatenation; simple, models cross-modal features, but suffers from curse of dimensionality and fails on missing modalities); Intermediate fusion (latent representation learning; maps modalities into shared embedding space, highly robust, but less biologically interpretable); Late fusion (decision-level ensembling; highly tolerant to missing/unpaired data, but misses fine-grained cross-modal synergies).
  - *Tier 2 (Algorithmic Strategies):* Hierarchical modeling (aligns with biological dogma: DNA -> RNA -> Protein), biological network/graph methods, attention-based fusion, multi-view contrastive learning, and correlation-based methods.
- **Review method:** Systematic PRISMA 2020 protocol + complementary narrative scoping review for preprints. Databases: PubMed, Google Scholar, arXiv, bioRxiv. Search: 3,280 initial records -> 332 unique screened -> 111 full texts assessed -> **54 core studies included** for meta-analysis (Table 4), supplemented by a comprehensive taxonomy of cancer foundation models across omics, pathology, and radiology.
- **Paper structure:** §1 Introduction pp. 2–5 · §2 Methods pp. 5–7 (2.1 search pp. 5–6; 2.2 PRISMA screening pp. 6–7; 2.3 FM curation p. 7) · §3 Multimodal integration methods pp. 7–16 (3.1 early/intermediate/late fusion pp. 9–11; 3.2 advanced hierarchical/graph/attention methods pp. 11–16; 3.3 comparative appraisal Table 1 p. 16) · §4 Tools and data banks pp. 16–23 (TCGA, GEO, METABRIC, CPTAC, ICGC) · §5 Results and trends pp. 23–45 (5.1 ML/DL trends pp. 23–29; 5.2 reproducibility barriers pp. 29–30; 5.3 clinical translation pp. 30–31; 5.4 foundation model taxonomy & compute scaling pp. 31–44) · §6 Open challenges and future directions pp. 45–60 (6.1.1 data-centric challenges pp. 46–50; 6.1.2 model-centric challenges pp. 50–57; 6.2 future directions pp. 57–60) · §7 Conclusions p. 60 · References pp. 61–69.
- **Key findings / claims:**
  - Intermediate and attention-based fusions significantly outperform early concatenation and late voting in survival and metastasis prediction by learning joint cross-modal latent distributions.
  - The rise of biological Foundation Models (e.g., scGPT, OmniCLIP) enables zero-shot and few-shot transfer to rare cancers where labeled training cohorts are critically small.
  - Massive "Translational / Evidentiary Gap": >90% of current cancer AI publications rely on retrospective, single-center public cohorts (e.g., TCGA); prospective randomized clinical trials, real-world utility evaluations, and FDA/CE approvals for multimodal models are practically absent.
- **Author-stated limitations:** Extreme data heterogeneity and missing modality rates in real clinical workflows; lack of standardized preprocessing pipelines; high compute and memory barriers preventing clinical hospital deployment; deep models lack mechanistic biological interpretability; absence of prospective validation and health-economic assessments.
- **Limitations we see:** Highly exhaustive (69 pages, ~350 references), functioning as an encyclopedia rather than an empirical benchmark; reports secondary performance metrics without standardizing evaluation seeds or hardware costs across surveyed models; heavy focus on oncology limits direct transferability to non-biological big data domains without abstraction.
- **Datasets / benchmarks discussed:** The Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA), Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO), METABRIC, CPTAC, 10x Visium spatial transcriptomics, CCLE, ICGC, SEER, and MIMIC multimodal EHR records.

## Synthesis hooks

- **Theme placement:** Foundation models & LLMs (landscape-report branch B4) and Applications & Domains / Healthcare (branch B3) — bridges the algorithmic evolution from classical shallow learners to massive self-supervised foundation models in high-dimensional multimodal big data.
- **Agrees with:** AIR-1 on foundation model scaling challenges, computational costs, and the limits of purely statistical representations (pp. 31, 50); AIR-2 on the primary blocker being operational deployment and real-world implementation rather than raw model accuracy (pp. 30, 57); AIR-4 on data scarcity, privacy hurdles, and the need for synthetic data / federated learning in healthcare (pp. 48, 51); JoBD-2 on privacy-preserving collaborative learning across medical centers; TBD-4 on attention-based transformers capturing long-range dependencies across complex multi-channel inputs.
- **Contradicts:** The assumption in benchmark-focused papers (e.g., JoBD-3) that increasing model stack complexity equals real-world utility — AIR-3 explicitly shows that multi-model complexity often degrades clinical reproducibility and fails in prospective clinical validation (pp. 29–31).
- **Extends/enables:** Grounds the high-level foundation model concepts in AIR-1 and TBD-2 into concrete biological modalities (genomics, spatial transcriptomics, digital pathology); supplies the clinical and biological data bank context for the data governance and synthetic data discussions in AIR-4.
- **Unique contribution:** The only paper in the pool providing a formal two-tiered integration taxonomy (timing vs. computational algorithm) paired with an exhaustive taxonomy of biological foundation models (scGPT, OmniCLIP, Nicheformer) and a critique of the clinical "translational gap" in oncology AI.
- **Quotable line:** "The performance, reliability, and ultimate clinical utility of any AI model are inextricably bound to the fidelity, structure, and integrity of the data upon which it is trained. The most sophisticated algorithm cannot overcome the limitations of flawed or poorly harmonized inputs." (p. 46) · *alt:* "Bridging this gap will require prospective impact evaluations, integration of multimodal FMs into clinical decision-support tools, and formal assessment of how their use affects survival, toxicity burden, time-to-diagnosis, and healthcare efficiency at the system level." (p. 31)
