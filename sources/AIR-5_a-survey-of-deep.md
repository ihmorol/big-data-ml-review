## Content (full text — all specifics confirmed against PDF)

- **Problem / domain:** Deep multivariate time-series (MTS) research is (i) *fragmented* across tasks, architectures and evaluation practices and (ii) of *uncertain reproducibility* — reported gains depend on inconsistent datasets, undocumented preprocessing, incomplete hyperparameters, seeds and evaluation protocols (pp. 3–4). Domain framing: healthcare, finance, industrial monitoring, energy (pp. 2, 37–38).
- **Big-data context:** Scale is asserted and partly quantified — "tens of zettabytes annually from IoT devices alone" (p. 38) underlies the shift from classical statistics to data-driven learning. Dataset scale is concrete (Table 4, p. 22): SMD 1,416,825 timesteps × 38 variables, WADI 1,221,372 × 103, SWaT 944,919 × 51, SMAP 562,800 × 25, PSM 220,322 × 26, MSL 130,046 × 55; plus high-dimensional benchmarks Electricity (321 channels) and Traffic (862 channels). No cluster/big-data platform is used — the audit runs on a single NVIDIA Tesla V100-DGXS (32 GB VRAM), CUDA 12.2, Python 3.12.7 (p. 21); edge/resource-constrained deployment is flagged as the harder setting (p. 40).
- **ML methods / architecture:** Eight Axis-1 modeling paradigms (§3.2, pp. 10–15; Table 3 p. 12): Transformer-based (PatchTST, Crossformer, DLinear, iTransformer), graph neural networks (FC-STGNN, Lite-STGNN, directed hypergraphs, MoE-routed variants), state-space models (Mamba, MambaTS, TimeMachine, CMamba, MambaMixer), self-supervised contrastive (TS2Vec, TimeCSL, CaTT, CoST, TimesURL, TF-C), reconstruction/masked autoencoding (TimeMAE, Ti-MAE, SimMTM), diffusion (ARMD, CSDI, Diffusion Transformers, physics-informed), LLM-reprogramming (Time-LLM, GPT4MTS, aLLM4TS), and native TS foundation models (Chronos, TimesFM, MOIRAI, UniTS, TimeGPT) — the last two are deliberately separated (p. 11). Axis 3 design capabilities: scalability/efficiency, robustness, transferability, interpretability (pp. 18–20).
- **Review method:** Narrative survey with a three-axis *multi-label* taxonomy (Axis 1 paradigm "how" × Axis 2 task "what" × Axis 3 capability "why"; §3.1 p. 10; Fig. 2 p. 10) **plus a targeted reproducibility audit** (§4, pp. 20–36). Not a PRISMA/systematic review — no search protocol or inclusion counts are reported; instead Table 1 (p. 7) compares downstream-task coverage across 15 prior surveys and Table 2 (p. 8) summarises 13 of them (methods / key features / limitations). Audit: 8 representative models re-run from **official repositories** under one protocol (Table 6, p. 26) — seed 42 baseline, then 5 random-seed runs (aLLM4TS, CSL, TimeCMA) or 10 official-seed runs (Informer, FC-STGNN, TS2Vec, ARMD, TSCMamba); normalisation/split/hyperparameter source recorded per model. Reproducibility is graded by a descriptive **1σ/2σ decision rule** (high / intermediate / low), explicitly *not* a formal significance test (pp. 23–24).
- **Data:** 17 benchmark resources catalogued in Table 4 (p. 22 — PSM, SMD, MSL, SMAP, SWaT, WADI, ETTh/ETTm, Electricity, Traffic, Weather, Exchange, Solar, ILI, HAR/UCI, UCR-26, UEA-30) plus C-MAPSS (Table 11, p. 30). Metric suite (p. 22): MAE/MSE/RMSE/MAPE/sMAPE (forecasting, imputation); Accuracy/F1 plus AUC-ROC/AUC-PR (classification, anomaly detection); ARI/NMI/Silhouette (clustering); InfoNCE/reconstruction error/CKA (representation, transfer).
- **Key findings (with numbers):**
  - **Reproducibility is task-dependent, not family-dependent.** aLLM4TS is "broadly reproducible for anomaly detection at the aggregate level, but its reliability is uneven across datasets" — MSL F1 falls 82.26 → 79.25±0.40 and SMD recall 83.09 → 80.44±1.18 (low) while SWaT F1 (94.57 → 94.67) and PSM F1 (97.19 → 96.91) remain high (Table 7, p. 27); its **classification gap is the clearest** — EthanolConcentration 33.50 → 27.00±2.36, UWaveGestureLibrary 85.30 → 77.19±3.83, Heartbeat 78.00 → 74.44±0.89 (all low; Table 8, p. 27); imputation is mostly high or intermediate across masking ratios (Table 9, p. 28).
  - **Broad trends reproduce; exact numbers often do not.** Informer: 7 of 40 dataset–horizon–metric settings are low, concentrated in ETTh1/ETTh2 at long horizons (ETTh2 h48 MSE 1.457 → 2.408±0.249), so the failure is configuration-specific rather than a paradigm failure (Table 10, pp. 29–30). TimeCMA is the worst case — all 16 dataset–horizon settings are low (e.g. ETTh2 h96 MSE 0.286 → 0.330±0.005) (Table 16, pp. 34–35). TSCMamba shows large classification gaps (EigenWorms 87.00 → 42.824±1.414%; Handwriting 53.30 → 21.976±1.318%) (Table 17, p. 36); CSL clustering is highly unstable across seeds (AtrialFibrillation NMI 0.587 → 0.129±0.024; StandWalkJump NMI 0.554 → 0.113±0.007) (Table 12, p. 31); TS2Vec is mostly high for classification but its Electricity forecasting collapses (h24 MSE 0.287 → 1.289±0.142) (Tables 13–14, pp. 32–33). FC-STGNN's C-MAPSS FD001 RMSE rises 11.62±0.19 → 12.39±0.35 (just past the 2σ threshold) (Table 11, p. 30).
  - **Six recurring reproducibility barriers** (Table 18, pp. 37–38): hyperparameter sensitivity; incomplete implementation details (normalisation, padding, splits); residual stochasticity despite fixed seeds (nondeterministic GPU ops / data-loading order); random-seed sensitivity; computational constraints (some long-horizon runs needed multiple high-memory GPUs and days); and evaluation-protocol variability. aLLM4TS forecasting could not be fully reproduced because of compute cost (p. 35), and TSCMamba's InsectWingbeat is "N/A, Not available" on preprocessing-cost grounds (Table 17, p. 36).
  - **Foundation models add leakage risk:** large TS foundation models may have been pretrained on public data overlapping benchmark test sets, which "can artificially inflate reported performance" (p. 40).
- **Author-stated limitations (§7, pp. 46–47):** deliberately deep-learning-centric — classical similarity/statistical/indexing methods are "mainly discussed for historical context"; the audit is "intentionally limited in scope" (a targeted empirical study, not a comprehensive benchmark, and not every taxonomy task is evaluated standalone); and the fast-moving field may outdate the taxonomy. The paper also states the 2σ rule is descriptive and that a formal paired test "is generally not possible" because original studies release only aggregate metrics (p. 24).
- **Limitations we see:** no formal search protocol, PRISMA flow or corpus-size count, so the "fragmented literature" claim rests on a narrative scan (contrast AIR-1/AIR-2/AIR-3, which report counted corpora); the audit covers 8 models on a single GPU with seed 42 as baseline, so a "low reproducibility" verdict blends the original method with the auditors' own environment; the protocol is internally inconsistent on clustering — §4.1 states results averaged over three runs (p. 21) while Table 6 lists five random-seed runs for CSL (p. 26); several low verdicts are threshold artefacts, e.g. ETTh2 25%-mask imputation is labelled low on a 0.046 → 0.047±0.000 change the authors themselves call "numerically minor" (p. 28); the Axis-3 radar (Fig. 3, p. 18) is explicitly qualitative; and benchmark contamination is named for foundation models but never tested.
- **Datasets / audited models:** audited — aLLM4TS (LLM-reprogramming), Informer (Transformer), FC-STGNN (GNN), CSL and TS2Vec (contrastive), ARMD (diffusion), TimeCMA (cross-modal), TSCMamba (SSM) (Table 5, p. 25). Baselines discussed but not audited: PatchTST, iTransformer, DLinear, Crossformer.
- **Table 1 row data:** Multivariate time series (forecasting, anomaly detection, classification, clustering, imputation, representation learning, transfer learning) | Transformers, GNNs, state-space/Mamba models, contrastive self-supervision, masked autoencoders, diffusion, LLM-reprogramming, native TS foundation models | Not reported — no big-data platform; single NVIDIA Tesla V100 (32 GB); streaming/edge deployment identified as the unsolved setting | 17 benchmark resources (Table 4) + C-MAPSS; largest SMD 1.42M × 38, WADI 1.22M × 103, Traffic 862 channels | Deep-learning-centric survey with a three-axis taxonomy that **empirically re-runs 8 official codebases under one protocol** and shows reported gains are often stable in trend but not numerically reproducible — the corpus's only reproducibility audit.

## Synthesis hooks

- **Theme placement:** Evaluation & reproducibility — branch B5 "Privacy, governance, and evaluation" (see `notes/landscape-report.md`). It is the pool's **only empirical reproducibility audit** and the evidentiary basis for the review's cross-cutting claim that the field's evaluation practice is fragmented.
- **Agrees with:** AIR-1 — both conclude that varied evaluation metrics across studies prevent direct cross-study benchmarking (AIR-1 p. 15; here §2.2.4, p. 9); AIR-2 and TBD-5 — deep reconstruction/forecasting methods dominate MTS anomaly detection and its evaluation is protocol-sensitive (point-adjustment "bubble scores," p. 41); AIR-3 — reproducibility barriers and benchmark-only evidence are the field's translation gap (pp. 30, 46); AIR-4 — fidelity/evaluation metrics do not track downstream usefulness, and evaluation needs multi-seed, attack-aware standards (pp. 6, 16, 53); JoBD-4 — streaming/real-time adaptation under latency is under-evaluated (§5.2.4, pp. 41, 46); BDR-2 — communication/energy cost and non-IID variance are the price of decentralised training (pp. 35, 37).
- **Contradicts:** the pool's default assumption that reported accuracy gains are reliable — this is the only paper that re-runs the code and shows single-run results overstate stability ("single-run evaluations often overestimate model stability," p. 4), which implicitly challenges every aggregate, self-reported number in AIR-2 and AIR-4; it also opposes the "deeper stack = better" assumption in benchmark-focused JoBD-1/JoBD-3 by showing large reported–reproduced gaps and, for TS foundation models, untested benchmark contamination (p. 40).
- **Extends/enables:** TBD-1, TBD-5, JoBD-5, BDR-3, BDR-5 (scalable/staged ML) — supplies the reporting standard (multi-seed variance, preprocessing disclosure, compute budget, rolling-window validation) those papers must meet; AIR-2 — turns its unattributed accuracy/latency ranges into a testable hypothesis (computational-constraint barrier, p. 37); AIR-4 — gives its qualitative rubric an outcome measure (does the claimed gain reproduce?); BDR-1/BDR-4 — frames irreproducibility as a deployment/risk hazard in high-stakes domains (pp. 37–38).
- **Unique contribution:** the pool's only paper that operationalises a taxonomy into an **empirical audit of 8 official codebases** with per-seed standard deviations and a formal (if descriptive) 1σ/2σ decision rule (Tables 5–6, pp. 25–26), and the only source of direct evidence on the reproducibility of deep MTS models across seven task families.
- **Quotable line:** "while broad performance trends are often reproducible, exact numerical results can vary across models, datasets, and protocols, particularly when preprocessing or seed-level details are underspecified." (p. 2) · *alt:* "These results indicate that single-run evaluations often overestimate model stability and highlight the need for routine variance reporting." (p. 4) · *alt:* "reported performance gains may be influenced by experimental variance rather than purely architectural improvements." (p. 36)
